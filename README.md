# OpenCyber Academy

**Learn cybersecurity through open lessons, worked examples and practice you can explain.** OpenCyber Academy is a free public learning library for cybersecurity, information security, privacy and digital defense. Read on the [study site](https://opencyber.academy/) or follow the same curriculum in this GitHub repository. Public reading does not require an account.

## What you can do here

- **Build understanding before using tools.** Lessons explain a concept, its assumptions and limits, then work through an example so you can see how a decision follows from evidence.
- **Practice and check your reasoning.** Published teaching links exercises to worked answers. Some lessons include safe, reproducible labs or fictional cases; they do not require probing real systems.
- **Follow a route without losing your place.** Course indexes distinguish authored lessons from outlines. Lesson navigation points to the previous step, the index and the next step; you can keep a simple note of your course, language and last exercise.
- **Study and reuse open material.** The books are public Markdown, readable on GitHub and through the study site. Maintainer-owned original material is dedicated under [CC0 1.0](RIGHTS.md), subject to the rights of cited third-party sources.

This library is for self-directed learners, working practitioners, teachers and study groups. Its courses are educational pathways; they do not confer an accredited qualification.

## How the learning path works

1. Choose a subject and a level, then open that course's language edition.
2. In `SUBJECTS.md`, follow the link to a published lesson in `book/MANUSCRIPT.md`. A `book/PLAN.md` is an outline, not teaching you have already read.
3. Read the explanation and worked example. Try the exercise on your own before comparing its worked answer.
4. Follow **Continue studying** to the next authored lesson. If the next step is still a plan, stop there or choose another published course.

Each course has one principal book per language, with a course index and supplementary [practice playbooks](playbooks/README.md). The [AI reading guide](AI_GUIDE.md) explains how an assistant should follow the same route without treating plans as lessons. The [curriculum grid](CURRICULUM_GRID.md) and [book standard](BOOK_STANDARD.md) describe the teaching and review goals.

## Languages and availability

English is the reference language for current writing and review. Portuguese editions contain existing material and are expanded from reviewed English units; Spanish editions also contain existing material, with broader planned translation following the Portuguese collection. Other language routes may contain outlines without translated teaching. **Choose the edition's course guide and check each lesson's publication state before studying.** A translated menu or a linked plan does not mean the whole book is available in that language.

For readers who want the editorial detail, see the [lesson inventory](LANGUAGE_LESSON_PROGRESS.md), [translation record](TRANSLATION_PROGRESS.md), [writing progress](WRITING_PROGRESS.md) and [review standard](BOOK_STANDARD.md). These records describe work in progress; they do not replace the lesson links above.
